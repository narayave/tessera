# Tessera

**A live territory game for a room full of strangers.**

Twenty-odd people walk into a party. Most of them don't know each other. Forty-five
minutes later they've claimed a map together, and every tile on it is a piece of
somebody's life.

Tessera is the engine. You bring the people and the content.

---

## The name

A *tessera* is a single tile of a mosaic — one small piece that means nothing
alone and everything in aggregate.

It's also a *tessera hospitalis*: a Roman token of friendship, deliberately
broken in two. Each person kept half. Years later, strangers could prove the
bond their families had made by fitting the halves back together.

Both meanings are the game. Tiles you claim together, and fragments that only
mean something when the people holding them find each other.

---

## What it actually is

A single continuous game, played on phones, against one shared map.

- **One board.** Not a sequence of mini-games. The map is up the whole night and
  every action feeds it.
- **Nothing is solvable alone.** Every tile needs several people holding
  different pieces of information. The blocker is always social — go find
  someone — and never intellectual.
- **Nobody is ever eliminated.** Losing means merging. The game structurally
  converges on the entire room being one team.
- **Content comes from the guests.** People answer a few questions before the
  event. Those answers become the board. Swap the answers, get a different party.

Built for house parties, but the engine doesn't care: it works for any group of
15–40 people who need to stop being strangers.
